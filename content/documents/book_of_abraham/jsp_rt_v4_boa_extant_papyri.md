## Abstract

*The Joseph Smith Papers, Revelations and Translations, Volume 4: Book of Abraham and Related Manuscripts* (JSP R&T 4) documents the physical and textual relationships between the extant papyri—specifically the *Breathing Permit of Hôr*—and the early Book of Abraham manuscripts. This page compiles the volume's explicit editorial statements establishing those links.

---

## Editorial Statements from JSP R&T 4

### 1. Egyptian Alphabets and GAEL Characters Derived from the Extant Papyri

> "The characters in the Egyptian alphabets and the grammar and alphabet book were copied from the margins and text of the Egyptian papyri in the possession of Joseph Smith, specifically the Breathing Permit of Hor."

— **JSP R&T 4**, *Part 3: Egyptian Alphabet Documents / Volume Introduction*

This statement directly ties the characters in the Kirtland-era Egyptian Alphabet documents and the *Grammar and Alphabet of the Egyptian Language* (GAEL) to the surviving *Hor Book of Breathings* fragment held in the Church History Library.

---

### 2. Marginal Characters in Book of Abraham Manuscripts Correlated with English Text

> "Characters taken from the Egyptian papyri appear frequently in the left margins of the Book of Abraham manuscripts, aligned with specific lines or paragraphs of the English text. These marginal references demonstrate an active, ongoing effort by the scribes to link segments of the dictated English text with specific characters from the characters extracted from the papyrus collection."

— **JSP R&T 4**, *Part 4: Book of Abraham Manuscripts / Volume Introduction*

The editors document that characters from the extant papyrus fragments were written alongside the English translation in both the Kirtland (1835) and Nauvoo (1842) manuscript drafts (e.g., Manuscript C).

---

### 3. General Volume Introduction: Association of Extant Papyri with English Text

> "It is clear that Joseph Smith and/or his clerks associated the characters from the [surviving Breathing Permit of Hôr] papyri with the English Book of Abraham text."

— **JSP R&T 4**, *Introduction to Revelations and Translations, Volume 4*

This summary statement from the volume's general introduction frames the documentary consensus: the surviving papyrus fragments were the ones connected to the translation process.

---

## Synthesis

The editorial apparatus of JSP R&T 4 provides the primary empirical basis for the external commentary of editors Brian M. Hauglid and Robin Scott Jensen. The volume establishes three physical-textual links: (1) the Egyptian Alphabet/GAEL characters were copied sequentially from the *Breathing Permit of Hôr*; (2) characters from the extant fragments appear in the margins of Book of Abraham manuscripts aligned with English paragraphs; (3) the editors conclude that Smith and his scribes treated the surviving papyri as the source of the translation. Hauglid and Jensen's external statements—rejecting the "missing papyri" hypothesis—extend this documentary foundation by evaluating its implications for the translation's historicity.
